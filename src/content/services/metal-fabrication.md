## From drawing to finished part

Fabrication is a chain of steps, and a part is only as good as the weakest one. We manage the whole chain:

1. **Review.** We check your drawing for manufacturability and flag anything that will add cost or risk: tight tolerances across welds, inaccessible joints, or material combinations that complicate welding.
2. **Cutting.** Laser, waterjet, shear and saw cutting, sized to the material and tolerance.
3. **Forming.** Bending and forming of sheet, plate and tube.
4. **Fit-up and welding.** Fixtured assembly and [precision TIG welding](/services/tig-welding/) (or MIG, where the part calls for it), with distortion control built into the sequence.
5. **Machining.** Machining of components before welding, or of welded assemblies afterward, where features must be held to tolerance.
6. **Finishing.** Grinding and polishing, passivation, powder coating, anodizing or plating.
7. **Inspection and delivery.** Checked against your drawing and delivered with the documentation you need.

## Tolerances on fabricated parts

Welding moves metal, so fabricated parts are toleranced differently from machined parts. For good results at a sensible cost:

- **Put tight tolerances where they matter.** Hold critical features and interfaces tight, and let general dimensions take standard fabrication tolerances.
- **Machine after welding when precision is critical.** Bores, mounting faces and datum features that must be accurate relative to each other are often best machined after the weldment is complete.
- **Define datums that make sense for a weldment.** Choose datums on features that stay stable through welding, not on surfaces that will move.
- **Talk to us early.** A short review of the drawing before release often saves significant cost and rework.
