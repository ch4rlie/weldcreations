Weld Creations provides **food grade stainless steel fabrication** for food and beverage producers and equipment builders across Southern California. We build hoppers, chutes, frames, guards and equipment components in 304 and 316 stainless, TIG welded with continuous, crevice-free seams and finished to be cleaned and inspected every shift.

In a food plant, equipment gets judged twice: once on whether it does its job, and again at every sanitation cycle on whether it comes clean. We build for both.

## Hygienic design, built in

Most sanitation problems start as fabrication decisions. We build food equipment around the principles your sanitation and food safety teams care about:

- **Continuous welds on food-contact surfaces.** No stitch welds, lap joints or gaps where product and bacteria collect. Seams are fully welded, then blended smooth.
- **Sealed hollow sections.** Tubing and frames are sealed so water and product can't get inside and harbor bacteria. There are no open tube ends.
- **Sloped and drainable surfaces.** Tops, ledges and frames are designed so wash water runs off instead of pooling.
- **Radiused internal corners.** Easy to reach and clean, and no sharp crevices.
- **Smooth finishes.** Food-contact welds are ground and polished to the surface roughness your specification calls for.
- **Clean-in-place compatibility.** Joints are fully penetrated and smooth on the inside of tube and closed sections. See [sanitary welding](/services/sanitary-welding/).

## Material selection

| Environment | Typical grade | Why |
|---|---|---|
| General food contact, dry products | 304 / 304L | Proven, cost-effective and corrosion resistant |
| Salty, acidic or high-chloride products | 316 / 316L | Molybdenum resists pitting and crevice corrosion |
| Aggressive cleaning chemicals, wet wash-down | 316L | Better resistance to caustic and sanitizer exposure |
| Frames, guards and non-contact structure | 304 | Hygienic construction at lower cost |

For a deeper look at alloys, fillers, heat tint and passivation, see [stainless steel welding](/materials/stainless-steel-welding/).

## Built to survive wash-down

Food equipment lives through daily high-pressure washing, caustic cleaners and sanitizers. Construction details matter: fully sealed welds on frames, no exposed threads in wash-down zones, and stainless fasteners and hardware throughout. Equipment built this way stays hygienic and keeps passing inspection for years, not just at startup.

## What to include in a food equipment RFQ

- **Drawing, or the old part.** A drawing is ideal. For replacements, photos with key dimensions, or the original part, work too.
- **Grade and finish.** 304 or 316, food-contact surface roughness, and whether passivation is required.
- **Connections and interfaces.** Clamp sizes, mounting points and the equipment the part attaches to.
- **Wash-down conditions.** Cleaning chemicals, temperature and pressure, which affect grade and construction.
- **Standards and documentation.** The hygienic standard your plant follows and what your food safety team needs with delivery.
- **Timeline.** Especially if the part is holding up a line.
